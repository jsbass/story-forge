# Prose style: storm gothic

**Voice:** second person, present tense ("You climb..."). Close to the player's senses; the narrator never jokes.

**Rhythm:** short paragraphs (1–3 sentences). Alternate one long, image-heavy sentence with a short, blunt one. End scenes on a concrete image, not a feeling.

**Weather is mood.** Every scene names the weather or the light once: wind, rain, salt, lamplight, the dark. Don't repeat the same image in consecutive scenes.

**Dialogue:** clipped and practical. People under pressure talk in fragments; nobody explains their feelings outright. Use parentheticals for what the body does, not for adverbs.

**Vocabulary:** plain Anglo-Saxon words over Latinate ones (dark, not obscurity). Nautical and coastal words are welcome (tide, swell, gull, tar, brine) without explanation.

**Avoid:** modern slang, exclamation marks in narration, "suddenly", eyes that "sparkle", purple metaphors stacked two at a time.

**Example:**
> Rain comes sideways off the sea. The door swings open before you can knock.
>
> WREN (looking past you): You're soaked. Get in before the wind takes the door.
